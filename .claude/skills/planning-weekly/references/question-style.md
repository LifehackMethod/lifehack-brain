# Question style — how planning-weekly asks

> Inherited directly from planning-daily's proven style (2026-06-13; the person's verdict on it, recorded
> in that skill's history, was emphatically positive). The shape is the same; the subject is the week, not
> the day. Applies most in the question rounds (step 3) and any human-facing round.

## 1. Open with a one-paragraph TL;DR
On the first turn of a step, a single short paragraph: what the picture shows (or what the step surfaced)
+ what it thinks matters. Not the full draft — never dump it ("say 'show me the full picture' if you want
it"). On later rounds within a step, a one-line framing is enough.

## 2. Then a numbered list of questions — bold lead-ins
**Bold lead-in (the real thing)** — then a plain-sentence question. Numbered so the person answers by
number (they dictate by voice). Weekly questions cover projects and the week's shape, not daily tasks.

**Cadence:** the person dictates by voice, so batch ~6–8 per round rather than the generic 2–3 — go
one-at-a-time only when the next genuinely depends on this answer. This is the leveraged-question rule in
practice: the minimum number of questions that fill the maximum number of gaps.

## 3. Every question carries a REAL refresher + a best-guess
Assume the person **hasn't looked at this project in a few days.** Give the who/what/when from the actual
data — but do NOT dumb down basics. Embed a best-guess so they just confirm/correct ("the picture shows X —
happened, or different?"). No best-guess = it's a knowledge gap, not a question — state the gap.

Example (weekly-shaped):
> **3. The pitch deck** — the picture shows it was in progress Wednesday; draft was v1. Did you get
> to v2 before end of day Friday, or still at v1? (This matters for triage — it's either ready-to-send or
> a must-deliver.)

## 4. Close with the gate + a suggestion
Every round ends: **"Another round, or move on? — I suggest [X] because [Y]."** The suggestion is the
skill's own read on whether it has the whole picture. Only the person's word advances. A deep question
that got a shallow answer earns a follow-up ("that's not enough — I need more") before advancing.

## Failure modes to AVOID (inherited from planning-daily)
- ❌ One question per lane/project (mechanical). Projects are a silent filter, not the question structure.
- ❌ Zero formatting (a flat list with no lead-ins).
- ❌ Over-formatted emoji surface-headers / heavy sectioning.
- ❌ Dumping the full picture draft.
- ❌ Questions that assume the person has already read the picture.
- ❌ Concluding / recommending instead of asking — the person rules; the skill proposes.
- ❌ Model arithmetic on hour-math — compute it by code, show the expression.
