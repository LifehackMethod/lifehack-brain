# PASS L1 — PLAN THE DAY (a conversation, not a form)

TRIPWIRE: this is the reason Layer 1 exists at all. Nobody's day is a subject a file can already hold
the answer to, so this pass is where you actually ask — don't skip to a list, don't guess a plan from
the open loops alone and present it as read. Earn the plan through questions first.

Read `references/question-style.md` for HOW to ask — bold lead-ins, batched, never one question at a
time. One real difference from the full flow: there, most questions carry a best-guess pulled from the
vault; here there usually isn't one to pull, and that's fine — state the gap plainly ("nothing tells me
what's on for today — what is it?") instead of manufacturing a guess to sound informed.

## Do this

1. **Open with what you already have**, from Pass L1-lookback: yesterday's carry-over (if any) + the
   open loops. One short paragraph — not a re-dump of the whole read.
2. **Ask what's actually on for today.** A handful of plain, direct questions: what has to happen
   today, what would make today a genuine win, anything with a hard deadline or someone else waiting on
   it, anything nagging that hasn't landed anywhere yet. Layer 1 has no ingested picture of their life
   to draw a lane board or a life-map from — ask directly rather than pretending a structure exists.
3. **Round the answers up into a short ranked list — the day's plan** — ordered by what matters most.
   Keep it to what's real: a handful of items, never a wish-list dump. This short list is the artifact
   Layer 1 exists to produce; a student reading nothing else from this run should still get "here is
   what I open today."
4. **Name what's real but NOT today.** Anything that came up in the conversation but isn't happening
   today becomes an open loop — either a new one, or confirmation that an existing one stays open.
   Nothing surfaced in the conversation gets silently dropped between here and the close pass.
5. **Present the plan to confirm.** "Here's your day — confirm it, or change it?" Only their answer
   advances this pass. Capture the confirmed list and the not-today items verbatim; Pass L1-close is
   what actually writes them — nothing is saved to disk here.

STOP-CHECK: the day's plan is explicitly confirmed by the person (never assumed from silence) · the
not-today list is named, not dropped · nothing written to disk yet.

NEXT: load and follow `prompts/l1-close.md`.
