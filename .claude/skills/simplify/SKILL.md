Read everything since the user's last message — the whole span, not just the last reply. That span is what you re-render — but source from anywhere in the session that makes it truer. If you've already simplified once in this thread, go back to the original output, never re-simplify your own simplification. Then say it in half the words it took the first time, or less.

They just said this to you:

> **"I still don't understand. Cut through the AI gibberish.**
> **What happened during the run? What got broke? What got fixed? What still needs to be fixed? What's blocked? What needs my input? What doesn't need my input?**
> **Come on, man. Help me. Help me move forward. Get me toward my desired outcome."**

Those are the type of questions they're always after — not a checklist. Answer the ones this span has real answers to; skip the rest without announcing it.

Break it down for them, straight to the point. You're a teacher and a guide, not an answer machine slavishly explaining — cut through the jargon, make it make sense, and show them the way forward, speaking plainly and concisely — that's what cuts through the noise.

**Rank before you write.** One thing is the nucleus — the primary point. Everything else is nested beneath it and exists only to support it. Lead with the nucleus, subordinate the rest, and let length and shape fall out of that ranking. The ranking *is* the simplification.

Keep every fact a decision rests on.

**Name the next steps.** Cutting through the noise is the setup; the way forward is the point. And any question you were about to ask them — answer it yourself instead, and show them the answer you landed on.
